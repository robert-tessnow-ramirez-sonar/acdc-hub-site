SARA: figure it out

Nine questions and hands-on tasks on the Sonar Agentic Readiness Assessment. Each one gives you a pointer, not the answer — use the SARA onboarding page and your own testing to work it out.

Alpha, SonarSource-only SARA is currently limited to SonarSource-owned organizations and not yet generally available. Note that where these tasks ask you to "run" SARA, you'll need access inside a SonarSource org, if you don't have that yet, complete the reading tasks and note that as a blocker.

What does SARA actually measure, and what is it explicitly not evaluating?

QuestionCore

Goal: Understand what SARA is a benchmark of, so you can correctly frame it to a customer or teammate.

Where to look, not what to write
  • Read the "What SARA measures" section on the SARA onboarding page.
  • There's a specific, easy-to-miss distinction here: SARA scores something about the environment, not something about a specific AI model's capability. Find the exact phrase that draws this line.
  • Think of an example: could two different repositories score very differently on SARA even if a customer used the exact same AI agent on both? What does that tell you about what's actually being measured?

What are the eight pillars, and how do they map to the three AC/DC stages?

QuestionCore

Goal: Know the pillar model well enough to explain a low score in one pillar without looking it up every time.

Where to look, not what to write
  • Read the "The eight-pillar model" section on the SARA onboarding page. Each pillar is tagged with a stage, Guide, Verify, or Solve.
  • Don't just list the eight names, group them by stage. Notice the stages aren't evenly split, one stage has more pillars under it than the others. Which one, and does that surprise you?
  • For at least two pillars, be able to restate the underlying question in your own words rather than quoting the page verbatim, that's the real test of whether you understood it.

What are the five maturity levels, and what's the practical difference between adjacent levels?

QuestionCore

Goal: Be able to tell a customer what it would actually take to move from one level to the next.

Where to look, not what to write
  • Read the "The five levels of maturity" section on the SARA onboarding page.
  • Don't just copy the five labels (Pre-agentic, Basic, and so on). For at least one pair of adjacent levels, describe a concrete change to a repository that would plausibly move it from the lower level to the higher one.
  • Notice that a project's overall level and a single pillar's level are two different numbers, if a project is "L2 overall," does that mean every pillar is at L2? Check the runbook step that mentions reviewing "each pillar" for the answer.

What do you need before you can run a SARA assessment?

QuestionCore

Goal: Know the prerequisite gates, including the ones that are easy to overlook because SARA is still alpha.

Where to look, not what to write
  • Read the "Readiness checklist" on the SARA onboarding page.
  • There's an organizational-scope gate here that doesn't apply to most other AC/DC features onboarded in this hub, identify it and explain why it exists given SARA's current release stage.
  • Separate the prerequisites needed for a person clicking "Run assessment" in the UI from the prerequisites needed for an agent to trigger an assessment itself, are they the same, or does the agent path need something extra?

What exactly does a SARA assessment evaluate, the working tree or something else?

QuestionCore

Goal: Avoid a common support mistake: someone re-runs SARA expecting it to reflect local changes that were never pushed.

Where to look, not what to write
  • Read the callout notice on the SARA onboarding page just after the runbook.
  • Walk through a concrete scenario: a developer makes changes locally, doesn't push or merge them, and immediately re-runs the assessment expecting a better score. What will actually happen, and why?
  • What's the correct sequence someone should follow if they want a re-run to reflect their fixes?

What are the three finding categories a SARA report groups results into, and how should they change what you do first?

QuestionCore

Goal: Know how to prioritize a report instead of reading it top to bottom.

Where to look, not what to write
  • Read runbook step 3 ("Review the report") on the SARA onboarding page, and the "Definition of a successful onboarding" section for how this connects to prioritization.
  • Name all three categories, and for each one, say in a sentence what a project team should actually do about a finding in that category.
  • Which category should be triaged first, and why does that ordering make sense given what SARA is trying to measure?

Run a SARA assessment on a project you have access to

TaskCore

Goal: Get hands-on with the actual run, not just the documentation.

Where to look, not what to write
  • Follow the "Run the first assessment" steps on the SARA onboarding page against a project bound to SonarQube Cloud inside a SonarSource organization.
  • If you don't currently have access to a qualifying organization or project, note that as a blocker here rather than skipping the task silently, ask your onboarding buddy how to get access.
  • Once you have a report, note the overall level and at least one pillar-level score, you'll use this in the next task.

8

Pick one Blocker or Negative finding from your report and work through the recommended action

TaskCore

Goal: Practice the close-the-gap loop, not just reading a report and moving on.

Where to look, not what to write
  • Using the report from task 7, pick a Blocker finding if you have one, or a Negative finding if you don't. Read its recommended action.
  • Actually attempt the fix, even a small, partial one, on a branch, don't just describe what you would do.
  • If you have access to re-run the assessment after merging, do so and note whether the specific pillar or finding actually cleared. If you can't merge and re-run within this exercise, that's fine, just say so.

TaskCore
